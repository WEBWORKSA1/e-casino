What this simulator proves

Betting systems change the shape of your results, never their average. Martingale turns a steady small loss into many small wins and a rare, catastrophic loss. Paroli does the reverse. Over enough spins every system converges on the same thing: total amount wagered × house edge.

Expected loss = total wagered × house edge Martingale example (European red, $10 base, 7 straight losses = $1,270): P(7 losses in a row) = (19/37)^7 ≈ 0.94% → EV per series ≈ 0.9906 × $10 − 0.0094 × $1,270 ≈ −$2.05

Run Martingale with a $500 bankroll and look at the “sessions ending ahead” number: it is high. Then look at the average result: it is negative. That gap is the whole trick. Read the full breakdown in The Martingale System: Why Doubling Up Fails and Betting Systems Tested.

Reality check: no sequence of bets on a negative-expectation game can produce a positive expectation. The simulator uses virtual money so you can learn this for free.
