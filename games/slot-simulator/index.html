RTP is a promise about millions of spins, not yours

RTP (return to player) is the share of all money wagered that a game is designed to pay back over its lifetime. A 96% game keeps 4% on average: $4 of every $100 wagered. But you don't play the lifetime of the machine. You play a few hundred spins, and that is where volatility takes over.

Expected loss = bet × spins × (1 − RTP) $1 × 500 spins × (1 − 0.96) = $20 expected — but the spread around $20 is enormous.

Low-volatility games hand back small amounts often, so your balance drifts down slowly. High-volatility games pay rarely but big, so most sessions lose faster while a few win big. Both end at the same average. Learn more in RTP vs. Volatility and The Near-Miss Effect & Losses Disguised as Wins.

Tip: watch the “wins that lost money” number. When a spin pays $0.50 on a $1 bet, the machine celebrates — but you are down $0.50. Researchers call these losses disguised as wins.
