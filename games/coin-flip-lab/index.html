Streaks are normal. “Due” is not.

A fair coin has no memory, so the chance of heads on the next flip is 50% no matter what came before. Yet long streaks are far more common than intuition expects: in 100 flips a run of six or more identical results is the usual outcome, not a rare one. Gamblers who see such a run often conclude the other side is “due” — the gambler's fallacy.

P(heads | any previous sequence) = 1/2 P(5 heads in a row) = (1/2)^5 = 1/32 ≈ 3.1% — but over 100 flips there are dozens of chances for it to start.

Human-made sequences give themselves away: they switch sides too often and avoid long runs. A real coin switches about half the time and produces streaks that look “suspicious”. Roulette wheels and dice behave the same way — see the Roulette Simulator.
