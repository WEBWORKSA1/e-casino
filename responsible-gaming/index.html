Play Safe

Gambling should be paid entertainment with a known price. When it stops feeling that way, help is free, confidential and available right now.

Free, confidential help — 24/7

If you are in immediate danger or thinking about suicide, call your local emergency number now.

United States

National Problem Gambling Helpline

Call or text 1-800-MY-RESET (1-800-697-3738). Chat online via the National Council on Problem Gambling. 24/7, free, confidential.

Gamblers Anonymous hotline: 855-2CALLGA (855-222-5542). Crisis support: call or text 988.

United Kingdom

National Gambling Helpline

Freephone 0808 8020 133, 24/7, or live chat with GamCare.

Self-exclude from all UK-licensed online gambling with GAMSTOP. Samaritans: 116 123.

Australia

Gambling Help Online

Call 1800 858 858, 24/7, or chat at gamblinghelponline.org.au.

Free and confidential counselling for gamblers and families.

Canada

Provincial help lines

Support is run by each province. In Ontario, ConnexOntario: 1-866-531-2600.

Elsewhere, search “problem gambling help” plus your province for the official line.

Worldwide

Gambling Therapy

Free online support in many languages, including live chat and peer forums: gamblingtherapy.org.

Meetings worldwide: Gamblers Anonymous.

Tools that put you in control

⛔

Self-exclusion

Ask operators to refuse your business for a fixed period — one venue, one site, or every licensed online operator in your country (for example GAMSTOP in the UK). Many programs cannot be cancelled early, which is the point.

🧱

Blocking software

Apps such as Gamban or the free BetBlocker block gambling sites and apps across your devices.

🏦

Bank gambling blocks

Many banks let you block card payments to gambling merchants in their app. Some add a cooling-off delay before the block can be removed.

⏱

Limits & reality checks

Licensed operators must offer deposit limits, loss limits, session reminders and time-outs. Set them before you play, not during.

💵

Budget like a ticket

Decide the price of the night out in advance. Our expected-loss calculator shows what a session really costs.

🗣

Tell someone

Secrecy feeds gambling problems. Telling one trusted person — or a helpline adviser — is often the most powerful first step.

Warning signs

  • Spending more money or time than you planned, again and again
  • Chasing losses — gambling more to win back what you lost
  • Borrowing, using credit or selling things to gamble
  • Hiding gambling or lying about it
  • Feeling restless or irritable when trying to cut back
  • Gambling to escape stress, boredom or low mood
  • Missing work, study, sleep or family time because of gambling

Worried about someone else?

You can call the same helplines for advice as a family member or friend. Some practical steps:

  • Talk when things are calm, with specific examples and without blame.
  • Protect your own finances: separate accounts, no co-signing loans.
  • Avoid paying off gambling debts for them — it often delays recovery.
  • Look after yourself: support groups for affected others exist.

Frequently asked questions

Is gambling help really free and confidential?

Yes. The national helplines listed on this page are free, confidential and staffed by trained advisers. You don't have to give your name.

What is self-exclusion?

Self-exclusion is a formal request that gambling operators refuse your business for a set period. It can cover a single venue, every licensed online operator in a country, or both.

Can I get help for someone else's gambling?

Yes. Helplines support family members and friends too, and many offer dedicated counselling and peer groups for people affected by someone else's gambling.
